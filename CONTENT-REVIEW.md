# Content review — 26 October 2026

Reviewed all 38 existing multiple-choice questions, 29 cards, seven free responses, the guide, and the existing test-review material. Added 30 original MCQs and five original FRQs, keeping the original array positions for saved-progress compatibility.

## Material corrections

- ATP hydrolysis: distinguished net reaction energy from the energy required to break a bond; qualified −7.3 kcal/mol as a biochemical standard value.
- Enzyme inhibition: distinguished the pure noncompetitive model from all allosteric inhibition; explained that lower Vmax alone cannot identify the mechanism.
- Cofactors: clarified that coenzymes are organic cofactors, rather than treating all cofactors as inorganic.
- Reaction-rate plateaus: added equilibrium, inhibition, and inactivation as alternatives to substrate depletion.
- Temperature: removed universal claims about exact human and thermophile enzyme optima.
- Photosynthesis evolution: separated early prokaryotic photosynthesis from later oxygenic cyanobacterial photosynthesis.
- Photosynthesis disruptions: separated the direct effects of a proton leak from blocking electron transfer, and noted residual pools and cyclic flow.
- Test-review mass question: controlled for water content; wet mass loss alone cannot identify respiration.
- Existing 2021 FRQ explanation: clarified fermentation's NAD+ regeneration role and possible non-sugar respiratory fuels. Clarified the scoring point for ATP yield and the accepted no-resveratrol control. Replaced ambiguous “no increase” with the expected decreased ATP prediction.
- Removed an unsupported guarantee that the practice questions will appear on the student's next test.
- Preserved class topic labels but disclosed the changed current College Board numbering and the site's limited scope.

## Question design

New practice includes experimental controls, replication, interval-rate calculations, activation-barrier reading, limits of inference from kinetic data, chemical coupling, isotopic tracing, carbon accounting, and causal predictions. Every MCQ has four choices, a keyed answer, and an explanation. Models use illustrative values rather than claiming measured biological predictions.
